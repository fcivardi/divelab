# DiveLab Manifesto

## Teaching Control Engineering Through a Living Physical System

**Version 1.0**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Why DiveLab Exists

Control Engineering is one of the most beautiful areas of engineering,
yet it is often one of the hardest to learn.

Students are frequently introduced to differential equations, Laplace
transforms, state-space models, observers, Kalman filters, and optimal
control through a sequence of unrelated examples. DiveLab replaces this
fragmented approach with a single coherent physical system: **a scuba
diver underwater**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. A Single System, Many Phenomena

Within this one system we naturally encounter:

-   Hydrostatics
-   Buoyancy
-   Compressible volumes
-   Nonlinear dynamics
-   Stable and unstable equilibria
-   Positive and negative feedback
-   Delays
-   Sensors
-   State estimation
-   Kalman filtering
-   Frequency response
-   State-space models
-   Human control
-   Actuator saturation
-   Resource management
-   Multi-timescale dynamics

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Why Scuba Diving?

Every important variable has an immediate physical meaning: depth,
pressure, buoyancy, breathing, gas consumption, and vertical velocity.

Every equation explains something that students can visualize.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Learning Through Curiosity

DiveLab begins with questions, not equations.

Only after physical intuition is established do mathematics and
simulation appear.

The educational sequence is:

**Observation → Physical Model → Mathematics → Simulation → Insight**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. One Continuous Narrative

Pressure leads to buoyancy.

Buoyancy leads to force balance.

Force balance leads to dynamics.

Dynamics leads to instability.

Instability requires feedback.

Feedback requires sensing.

Sensing motivates estimation.

Estimation introduces observers.

Observers lead naturally to the Kalman filter.

Control leads to dive computers.

Dive computers lead to hidden physiological states.

Each notebook answers one question while creating the next.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Simulation as Experiment

Python is not the objective.

Python is the laboratory where dynamical systems come alive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. The Human Remains Part of the System

The diver often remains the controller.

The actuators are breathing, the BCD, and body motion.

DiveLab therefore introduces students to both automatic control and
human-in-the-loop systems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. A Course About Systems

Despite its name, DiveLab is not primarily about scuba diving.

Scuba diving is the medium.

The real subject is **systems thinking**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Educational Philosophy

Understanding grows from coherence.

Studying one physical system deeply is often more valuable than studying
many disconnected examples.

The objective is not merely to solve equations.

The objective is to think like a systems engineer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Vision

DiveLab aims to become:

-   an open educational resource;
-   a university-level course;
-   a Quarto book;
-   a collection of interactive Jupyter notebooks;
-   a reference for teaching systems thinking;
-   a contribution to engineering education research.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Core Principle

> **A single physical system, explored deeply, can reveal the universal
> principles of dynamical systems and control engineering.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# DiveLab Motto

> **Learn one system deeply. Understand many systems forever.**
